PEER-REVIEWED PAPER2022 · Nature Food

Xia, L. et al. · Global food insecurity and famine from reduced crop, marine fishery and livestock production due to climate disruption from nuclear war soot injection

Models six stratospheric soot-injection scenarios and estimates crop and fishery output and food-calorie availability. Central source for the scenario anchors used in the simulator.

Use carefully: estimates depend on assumptions about soot forcing, climate and crop response, trade, food distribution, livestock and adaptation. Not a deterministic forecast of deaths from any future conflict.

02
PEER-REVIEWED PAPER2019 · JGR: Atmospheres

Coupe, J., Bardeen, C. G., Robock, A. & Toon, O. B. · Nuclear Winter Responses to Nuclear War Between the United States and Russia in WACCM4 and ModelE

Compares two comprehensive climate-model simulations of a high-soot war scenario; examines temperature, precipitation and atmospheric soot lifetime.

Use carefully: the paper discusses major uncertainties in the amount, height and behaviour of fire-generated soot. A model experiment represents a specified scenario.

03
ASSESSMENT REPORT2021 · IPCC AR6 WGI

IPCC · Chapter 11: Weather and Climate Extreme Events in a Changing Climate

Assesses observed and projected heat extremes, heavy precipitation, pluvial and river floods, droughts, storms and compound events, including regional confidence and event attribution.

Use carefully: assessed confidence varies by hazard and region. Attribution assesses changes in probability or magnitude; it does not simplify every event to one cause.

04
ASSESSMENT REPORT2022 · IPCC AR6 WGII

IPCC · Chapter 4: Water

Assesses observed and projected water-cycle changes, water security, floods, droughts, ecosystem flows and water-related adaptation. Supports the comparison of flood risk and adaptation context.

Use carefully: the 4–5× direct flood-damage comparison at 4°C versus 1.5°C is an assessed global result with medium confidence, not a universal local damage multiplier.

Read chapter ↗
05
ASSESSMENT REPORT2022 · IPCC AR6 WGII

IPCC · Chapter 10: Asia

Regional synthesis of heat, monsoon, drought, floods, glacier and snow changes, urban exposure and adaptation options across Asia, including the Hindu Kush Himalaya and major South Asian river basins.

Use carefully: regional patterns do not replace catchment-scale, slope-scale or city-scale hazard assessment, especially where observations are sparse.

06
GLOBAL CLIMATE REPORTPublished 23 March 2026 · WMO

World Meteorological Organization · State of the Global Climate 2025

Reports the global temperature status, ocean heat content, sea level and other indicators for 2025, alongside impacts associated with extreme events.

Use carefully: global mean indicators are context for climate-system change, not site-specific forecasts of rainfall, flood depth or casualties.

TERMINOLOGY & RISKUNDRR terminology

United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction · Hazard

Defines hazards and describes hydrometeorological hazards of atmospheric, hydrological or oceanographic origin, including cyclones, floods, drought, heatwaves, cold spells and coastal storm surges.

Use carefully: terminology is a classification aid; local risk analysis still requires exposure, vulnerability and capacity data.
