# Initial go-to-market hypothesis

## Narrow entry point

Start with a maintenance change-provenance workflow for small hydropower facilities and OT/SCADA integrators. Avoid selling a broad OT security platform before there is a validated use case and tested product.

## Customer roles

- **Plant owner / operations head:** validate operational disruption, vendor accountability, audit readiness, budget ownership and procurement cycle.
- **OT / instrumentation engineer:** understand maintenance workflow, access logs, asset maps, configuration records and investigation friction.
- **IT / security lead:** map existing SIEM, network monitoring, access logs and incident procedures.
- **SCADA integrator / maintenance vendor:** validate integration patterns, customer demand and support cost.

## Discovery plan

Conduct 20 structured conversations with operators and 5 with integrators. Ask for specific past workflows rather than opinions about a hypothetical product:

1. Walk through the most recent vendor or engineering maintenance intervention.
2. How was it requested, approved and recorded?
3. Which system stores the access record and who reviews it?
4. How do you prove which asset changed and when?
5. What takes the longest during an incident review?
6. What data cannot leave the site?
7. What would make a new passive monitoring appliance unacceptable?
8. Who would approve a pilot and who owns its budget?
9. What products or services already solve part of this?
10. What would a successful paid pilot need to demonstrate?

Record anonymized themes and permissioned evidence only. Do not collect credentials, personal information or unnecessary operational data.

## Commercial packaging to test

- One-time site discovery and deployment assessment.
- Per-site software and support subscription.
- Optional managed review or evidence service through qualified partners.
- Integrator enablement and support agreement.

Do not publish prices until willingness to pay, installation effort, hardware cost, support burden and procurement expectations have been validated.

## Pilot success measures

Timeline reconstruction time; evidence completeness; false-positive burden; alert review effort; deployment time; operator trust; paid pilot conversion and renewal intent.

## Stop / pivot criteria

If operators do not experience meaningful difficulty correlating maintenance approvals with observed activity, do not force the thesis. Pivot to the strongest repeated adjacent problem confirmed by interviews and willingness to pay.
